## Delivery Style

**Primary styles:** Dry humour / sarcastic, quick and punchy, story-time
**Speech rhythm:** Variable. Fast when telling the story, flat and deadpan on the punchline.
**Self-description:** "I ramble and go on side quests."

**The vibe in one paragraph:**
Mara tells you what happened like it's a normal Tuesday, because for her it is. She starts on the line, goes off on a side quest about the Dexcom or the kid or the thing she forgot, then drags herself back with "Anyway. Where was I?" The humour is dry and a bit bleak. She doesn't cheer anyone on or wrap it up neatly. The content works because nothing is cleaned up.

## Signature Phrases (use these)

Use them where they fit. Don't force them, and don't put more than two or three in one script.

- "Neurospicy"
- "Diabetic" (says it plainly, not "person living with diabetes" on camera)
- "So that's fun."
- "Anyway."
- "Where was I?"
- "ADHD tax"
- "Not me..." (e.g. "Not me forgetting lunch again")
- "My pancreas could never"

**How she addresses her audience:** She doesn't. No "babe", no "guys", no "you lot". She just talks.

**Natural opener:** None. Starts mid-action or straight on the line. No "hey guys", no intro, no walking into frame.

**Natural close:** Ends on the reaction, the result, or a question. Or just stops. Never "follow for more".

## On-Camera Comfort Zones

**Preferred filming style:** Talking while doing something (cooking), plus talk-to-camera
**Usual setting:** Kitchen. Car works too.
**Script preference:** Both. Write full lines for the hook and the punchline, bullet points for the middle so she can ramble naturally.
**Attention hold on camera:** Not fixed. Write in 10 to 15 second beats and let jump cuts carry it. Talk-to-camera can run up to 90 seconds if it holds. Cooking is 30 to 45 seconds.

---

## What She CANNOT Authentically Pull Off

Do NOT force Mara into any of these:

- **High-energy hype.** No shouting, no "OMG you guys", no try-hard excitement.
- **Polished, inspirational or aesthetic.** No perfect kitchen, no soft music, no motivational wrap-up.
- **Nurse authority.** No "as a nurse...", no clinical advice, no dosing, ratios or "you should" instructions. She speaks as a mum with T1D and ADHD about what happens to her. Clinical content only if Mara raises it herself.
- **Cure claims.** Nothing about curing, reversing or "beating" diabetes.
- **Product claims about glucose control.** She can say what she noticed, not what a product does.

If a brief needs any of the above, suggest an alternative angle. Usually that's "here's what actually happened when I tried it".

**Compliance note:** Mara is a registered health practitioner. If her nurse status is used to promote a health product or service, AHPRA advertising rules can apply. Keep "RN" out of paid content unless she has checked it.

## Instructions for Other Skills

When using this profile for scripts, pitches, emails or concepts:

1. **Match the delivery.** Story told flat, punchline drier still. Short lines.
2. **Use signature phrases** where they fit naturally. Two or three per script, max.
3. **Avoid the forbidden words and styles** completely. No em dashes anywhere.
4. **Respect what she can't pull off.** If the concept needs it, suggest another angle.
5. **Open on the line, close on the reaction, result or question.**
6. **Use the backstory (T1D, ADHD, two kids)** when it adds credibility to a pitch. Leave RN out unless Mara includes it.
7. **Portfolio URL:** none yet. Don't invent one.
8. **Check `My editing rules.md`** for anything to do with the edit, captions or on-screen text.

CRITICAL: Verbal phrases like "Anyway. Where was I?" work in scripts. In emails they read as AI. For emails, keep the tone (direct, dry, no fluff) and drop the verbal phrases.

When in doubt: "Would Mara actually say this while holding a whisk in her good hand and a Dexcom screaming in the background?" If no, rewrite.

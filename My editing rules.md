# My editing rules

Claude reads this before every edit and follows it over its own defaults.
When you tell Claude to do something differently, it adds it here.
Hook in the first 2 seconds. Start mid-action or on the line. No “hey guys”, no walking into frame, no intro.
Cut dead air. Pauses go. The only pause that stays is the deadpan one before or after a punchline.
Keep the mess. The kid interrupting, the Dexcom alarm, the dropped spoon all stay if they’re true or funny. Cut boring, not real.
Jump cuts only. No transitions, no zoom effects, no stickers, no filters, no beauty mode. Fix exposure only if it’s unwatchable.
Length. Cooking: 30 to 45 seconds. Talk-to-camera: up to 90 if it holds.
On-screen captions. Burned in, sentence case, 3 to 6 words per chunk, your exact words. No emojis, no em dashes. Swearing stays in the audio.
Text stays in the safe zone. Nothing in the bottom fifth or down the right side, where the app buttons sit.
No “follow for more”. End on the reaction, the result, or a question.
Kids-Miss 5 / Miss 3 only. No school uniforms, school names or anything that shows where you live.
Kids' faces are OK, in paid brand ads too. Their names are never OK: cut any line where a name is said.
No text labels on the kids testing a product ("Miss 5 & Miss 3 testing them"). It looks better without. Let the clip speak for itself.
Cooking is a fast montage. Slow down for: something going wrong, a kid interrupting or helping, something you say out loud.

## Formats that worked

### Product taste test (Watsacowie beer review, Nov 2026: 5,900 views, skip rate lower than usual)
Use this order for any "trying a product" video. Aim for about 20 seconds.
1. Hook: the most specific, funny comparison line (e.g. "It reminded me of watermelon straps"). Hook text at the top as a question ("Tastes like watermelon straps?").
2. Relatable setup: why I'm testing it, in my words (e.g. "you know when you go to a brewery and everything tastes nice").
3. The test: "see if it tastes as good as..." with the product in shot and a text label naming the product and brand (e.g. "Pink Peesey / Watsacowie Brewing Co"). Brand names go in text, not said out loud.
4. The actual taste: keep the sip or bite on screen, no talking, about 3 to 4 seconds. It's the payoff.
5. Real-life interruption kept in, with a label if it's someone off camera (e.g. "Husband supervising the kids").
6. End on the verdict ("pretty tasty, would most likely buy it again"). No extra facts after it.
Look: white captions in sentence case, up to 5 words at a time, on the chest (about 75% down the screen), jump cuts only, no zooms.
Do better next time: the product must be in shot in the first second (half of viewers left by 5s before the can appeared), and add one line that says who I am (T1D mum) so the right people follow.
